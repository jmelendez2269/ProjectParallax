# Project Parallax Newsletter Deliverables

## Purpose

Use Field Notes as the first low-friction doorway into Project Parallax.

The newsletter should not feel like a separate publication, a paid magazine, or another platform users have to understand before they understand the work. It should feel like an invitation to stay close while the products take shape.

## Recommendation

Do not start with Substack as the primary home.

Substack is useful when the newsletter itself is the product. Project Parallax is different: the newsletter is a trust layer that should bring people back to Kairos, Prismarium, Chronos, and the founder's perspective. Putting the reading experience on another branded platform adds friction before someone becomes a user, supporter, collaborator, or client.

Start with:

- Public posts on `projectparallax.xyz`
- Email capture embedded on the site
- An email provider behind the scenes
- No paid newsletter gate at launch
- Optional paid/support layer later through Ko-fi, Stripe, or product subscriptions

## Newsletter System

### 1. Field Notes

Public editorial posts that build trust, language, and worldview.

Cadence: 2 posts per month.

Role:

- Explain the philosophy behind the product family
- Show how the founder thinks
- Create searchable, shareable pages on the Project Parallax domain
- Feed social posts, product copy, launch emails, and future course material

First three posts:

1. Why personal astrology needs its own vocabulary
2. Seven lenses: what they are and why they matter
3. On building luxury software for the spiritual life

### 2. Dispatches

Short email notes sent to subscribers when a Field Note or product milestone goes live.

Cadence: 2 to 4 emails per month.

Structure:

- Opening note from the founder
- Link to the full Field Note
- One product update or behind-the-scenes note
- One invitation: reply, join waitlist, support, or try a product

### 3. Waitlist Segments

Use one list with simple interest tags.

Tags:

- `kairos`
- `prismarium`
- `chronos`
- `general`
- `supporter`
- `collaborator`

Signup copy should let people self-select without making the form complicated:

> Join Field Notes for essays, product updates, and early access across the Parallax universe.

### 4. Conversion Paths

Field Notes should create soft paths into the ecosystem.

- Kairos articles point to `kairosplanner.xyz`
- Prismarium articles point to `prismarium.xyz`
- Chronos articles point to the waitlist
- Design/founder articles point to support, collaboration, or email replies

## MVP Deliverables

### Week 1: Foundation

- Update homepage newsletter block from generic "subscribe" to Field Notes positioning
- Link the existing three article cards to the newsletter/waitlist until full article pages exist
- Choose one email provider
- Create the main list and interest tags
- Add a welcome email
- Create a simple private content calendar

### Week 2: First Content Drop

- Publish the first Field Note on the site
- Send the first Dispatch email
- Create 3 short social excerpts from the article
- Add "related product" CTA to the article

### Week 3: Product Signal

- Publish a Kairos or Prismarium behind-the-scenes note
- Add a waitlist question: "Which doorway are you most interested in?"
- Start tracking replies manually
- Use the response pattern to decide which product deserves the next launch push

### Week 4: Decision Point

Review:

- Subscriber growth
- Replies
- Clicks to Kairos
- Clicks to Prismarium
- Chronos waitlist interest
- Support/collaboration inquiries

Decision:

- If Kairos clicks and replies are strongest, start with Kairos onboarding and product education.
- If Prismarium clicks and replies are strongest, start with essays, library demos, and lens education.
- If Chronos waitlist interest is strongest, keep it in research mode but collect use cases.
- If design/founder essays perform best, use the newsletter as the parent brand channel first.

## Provider Direction

Best starting posture:

- Use a newsletter/email provider as infrastructure only
- Keep public posts on Project Parallax
- Avoid platform-first monetization
- Avoid paid newsletter language until the audience asks for deeper access

Recommended short list:

- Kit if creator-style publishing and tagging are the priority
- MailerLite if simple newsletters and landing pages are the priority
- Buttondown if a quiet, developer-friendly newsletter tool is the priority
- Substack only if the publication itself becomes the product

## Success Criteria

The newsletter is working if it helps answer:

- Which product do people understand fastest?
- Which doorway gets replies?
- Which ideas make people click?
- Which audience is forming first: planners, symbolic researchers, astrologers, designers, or supporters?

The first goal is not revenue. The first goal is signal.
