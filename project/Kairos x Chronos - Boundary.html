What this tells me. The split is real at the surface level — different jobs, different verbs, different reasons to open the app — but the bible is also right: under the hood, this is one product. Trying to ship them as two separate apps would force you to duplicate auth, the chart engine, the journal, the AI, the news pipeline. All of it. That's the boundary problem you've been feeling.

04Three ways to package this · pick one

Same architecture, three different go-to-market shapes. The technical answer is "one product, two surfaces." The strategic question is whether you tell users that.

my pick · A
one product · two stances · same shell

"Stelloquy" — and inside it, Kairos & Chronos are modes

One app. One login. Open it and there's a top-level toggle (or a tab pair, or a swipe): Kairos for today's sky · Chronos for your record. Same data underneath. The names live as stances — vocabulary the product teaches you, the way Apple taught us "Focus" or "Memoji." Your sightline mark can sit at the brand level. Each mode gets a tonal shift (cool/warm) but uses the same components.

// pros
  • matches the bible exactly
  • one codebase, one auth, one funnel
  • data flywheel never splits
  • users learn one app · two beautiful words
// cons
  • "why are there two names for one app?"
  • requires great UX to make the toggle feel meaningful, not nav clutter
option B
two apps · same account · shared backend

Kairos.app and Chronos.app — siblings, not the same app

Two distinct downloads, two URLs, two app-store listings. Same Stelloquy account underneath, same data. Like Google's "Calendar" and "Tasks" — clearly siblings, but each has its own front door. Stelloquy becomes the umbrella label that nobody really sees, similar to "Project Parallax" already.

// pros
  • each surface gets a focused identity
  • can market & price each separately if needed
  • one user can love one and ignore the other
// cons
  • 2× design surface, 2× shells, 2× onboarding
  • users will ask "do I need both?"
  • the loop you described (capture → feed insights to planner) gets weird across two apps
option C
one app · one name · k+c are internal vocabulary

Just "Stelloquy" — Kairos & Chronos become words inside the product

Drop the K and C as product names entirely. Keep them as system vocabulary the product teaches: you tap a button labeled "kairotic moment" or you look at your "chronos record." Like how Slack teaches you "channel" and "huddle" but doesn't sell them as separate products.

// pros
  • simplest possible go-to-market
  • matches the bible 1:1 · no rebrand
  • frees Kairos/Chronos to be poetic UI labels
// cons
  • you lose two beautiful brandable assets
  • the visual system you already built (sightline pair etc.) gets quiet
  • "Stelloquy" alone is a harder name to land
Why I'd pick A. It honors the bible (one product, one data flywheel) and uses the visual work you've done. Kairos and Chronos earn their names by being stances — different angles on the same self. Same architecture as Apple's Photos vs. Memories, or Linear's Inbox vs. Backlog: same data, two intentional ways of looking. It also matches the metaphor you said you loved: "Chronos is the horizon. Kairos is the moment something crosses it." A toggle is literally that — switching from looking along the horizon to looking at the strike.

05What I need from you to lock this

Before we touch any visuals — five questions to settle the boundary.

1. Pick a packaging shape — A, B, or C?
My read is A. But you live with this every day; if siblings (B) feels right because the audiences are different, that's a real signal.
2. Does Stelloquy stay the umbrella name?
Bible says yes. But "stelloquy" is also the AI voice in earlier rounds. Pick one job for that word.
3. Is the planner part of Kairos, or its own thing?
You said Kairos = "see your day/week/year." That's astrology-aware planning, which Co-Star and The Pattern don't do well. Could be your wedge.
4. Where does the AI live?
Both surfaces, or only Chronos (where the record is)? My read: both — but it speaks differently in each. In Kairos it's predictive ("this week will…"). In Chronos it's reflective ("last time you felt this…").
5. What's the single sentence for each?
e.g. "Kairos shows you what's lit up · Chronos remembers how you actually felt." Once we have these, every visual decision gets easier.
6. Are we re-running v4 visuals after this?
If A: we keep WM-05, sightlines, the conceptual core — and rebuild Kairos/Chronos as paired surface aesthetics, not paired logos.
